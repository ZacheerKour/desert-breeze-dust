# Phantom Dust recomp - retro, 2026-10-16/17

## Where it stands
- Boots in ~17-20 s on the D3D11 path (`RECOMP_HW=1`), 30 FPS in menus and the 3D Clinic hub (400-1150 draws).
- Working: title and menus, input (pad and keyboard), audio (menus, clicks, ambience, movie audio), XMV attract and opening movies, glow, stencil lighting, decals, brightness slider, window-driven render scale, custom soundtrack listing and preview (`make_soundtrack.py`), profile create/load, hub free-roam with stick movement.
- Not yet seen: a real battle, Split Screen battle (needs arsenals), battle music, most movies, Options > Details/Sound, long sessions.
- Known hazard: Scenario with a valid profile can exhaust the 64 MB contiguous arena after several video-mode changes and crash (pd-cha). `RECOMP_CONTIG_RECLAIM=1` is a partial fix, not verified.

## What changed this stretch (all under xboxrecomp/ and pd-game-v2/, with .bak files)
- Flicker: the second display buffer (0x80330000) is sometimes registered 640x512; the presenter showed it whole so the picture changed shape every other frame. Now cropped to 640x480 (nv2a_hw_d3d11.inc). The crop first failed because it compared texture size, not logical size, under render scale.
- Profiles: `Phantom Dust` profile is a blank save from before the 8-bit imul lifter fix; `_ERROR_` is the game's own fallback; a fresh profile (`h`) works. The "You could not be signed in" banner is the Xbox Live status line.
- Memory: found the arena leak (frame/Z buffer pair re-allocated on every video-mode change), added `RECOMP_CONTIG_RECLAIM` (+ `RECOMP_CONTIG_MIN`, `RECOMP_CONTIG_LOG`), bounds-checked the pushbuffer scanner.
- Debug aids: RECOMP_HW_FLIPDBG, RECOMP_SHA_DUMP, RECOMP_VM_LOG, RECOMP_FN_WATCH=3 + RECOMP_FN_ARGS, RECOMP_CONTIG_LOG; crash report now prints the RIP module offset. Scripts: dev/run_mp_boot.sh, dev/repro512.sh, mv.sh, tools/analysis/ext.py/ext2.py, tools/analysis/dis2.py.

## What went well
- Measuring before fixing paid off: capturing 10-12 window frames and comparing extents made the flicker objective; per-flip logs named the surface.
- Logging allocation sites (caller addresses) turned a vague "crash in the heap" into a precise leak story in about three runs.
- Env-gated diagnostics keep the default build unchanged.

## What went badly
- Chased symptoms first: early "white patches" and "dark decals" were blamed on the combiner when the causes were self-sampling render targets and missing stencil.
- Checked size/shape assumptions against the wrong units (texture vs logical size under render scale), so a correct idea shipped broken once.
- Blind stick walking in a fixed-camera hub burned many runs. Reading the event scripts or driving the story deterministically would have been cheaper.
- Several runs started from a stale env or a build that had failed (no rebuild check), producing misleading results; one 4-minute flow per hypothesis is slow.
- I could not confirm I had read the toolkit's GETTING_STARTED guide in full at the start; I read it only when asked. Local docs under xboxrecomp/docs (lessons-learned.md, indirect-calls.md) still deserve a pass.

## Lessons
- Always re-export RECOMP_* in each Bash call and confirm the build linked before running.
- Prove the bad frame/value exists (capture, log) before touching the presenter or lifter.
- Fixed sizes in the guest (the 44 MB pool at 0xB2920) mean our allocator must reclaim like the real kernel; "free is a no-op" bump allocators will always leak under a game that re-creates its device.
- Do not delete or move saves without approval; create new test profiles instead.
